## When a paraphrase still needs a citation

This is the part that decides whether the tool helps your writing or gets you into trouble.

A paraphrase is your wording of someone else's idea. The idea still belongs to the source. Pakistani universities, and most universities anywhere, treat an uncited paraphrase as plagiarism. Changing the verbs and moving the clauses around does not create a new idea. QuillBot will happily do that change. The citation is still yours to add.

Use it this way:

- You read a paper and write, in your own notes, what the author found.
- You run your note through the paraphraser if the English is stiff.
- You put the author's name and the year, or a footnote, next to the sentence in the assignment.
- You keep the original paper so you can check that the paraphrase did not twist the finding.

Do not use it this way:

- Pasting a classmate's assignment, a blog post, or a journal abstract, taking the rewrite, and submitting it as your work with no source.
- Asking the tool to "make this undetectable". Detectors and teachers both look at ideas, not only at word order. Our [student tools guide](/blog/best-ai-tools-for-university-students-pakistan.html) says the same thing: practising a rewrite is fair, disguising someone else's work is not.
- Running a whole essay through it the night before submission and never reading the result aloud. Paraphrasers drop qualifiers. "Some students reported" can come back as "students reported", and the meaning has changed.

If your department has an AI policy, read it before you use any writing tool on assessed work. Some courses allow grammar help and ban generated prose. QuillBot will not tell you which rule your teacher set.

The plagiarism checker is a warning light. It can show that a sentence matches a published source. It is not a letter from your university saying the assignment is clear. Run it, then cite or rewrite, then follow the department's own process.

## A practical way to summarise a long reading

Summaries are the cleanest use of the tool, because you are trying to understand a text, not to replace it.

1. Read the introduction and the conclusion of the article yourself. You need a human sense of the claim before a summary can be checked.
2. Paste one section, not the entire PDF library, into the summarizer.
3. Read the short version and mark anything that sounds stronger than the original. Summaries often drop "may" and "in this sample".
4. Write three sentences in your own notes: the question, the method, the result. Those three sentences are what you will actually remember.
5. If you use a sentence from the summary in an essay, cite the original paper, not QuillBot.

A summary is a map. The assignment still needs your judgement: do you agree, is the sample small, does this apply in Pakistan. The tool does not sit the exam for you.
