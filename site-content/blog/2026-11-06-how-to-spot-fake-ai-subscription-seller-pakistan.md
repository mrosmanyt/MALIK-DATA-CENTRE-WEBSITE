Buying ChatGPT, Canva Pro or CapCut from a reseller in Pakistan can save money and avoid an international credit card. The risk is not the idea of a reseller itself. The risk is paying a fake AI subscription seller who takes your money, disappears, or delivers something unsafe. This safety guide shows the red flags, the questions to ask, and a simple checklist you can use before you send a single rupee on EasyPaisa, JazzCash or USDT.

## What makes a seller fake?

A fake seller is anyone who takes payment for an AI or software plan and then fails to deliver, mislabels the access type, steals your login, or sells an offer the real company does not provide.

Honest resellers exist. They list clear PKR prices, say whether a plan is shared or private, send a setup guide, and honour a warranty. Fake sellers copy that language, then break trust after payment. Common patterns include:

- Taking money and blocking you on WhatsApp.
- Selling a shared login as a "private" account.
- Asking for your Gmail, Canva or OpenAI password.
- Advertising lifetime Canva, "GPT unlimited forever", or one cheap pack that unlocks many brand-name tools.
- Sending lookalike payment links that collect card details.

Canva's own help page on [fake Pro subscriptions](https://www.canva.com/help/fake-pro-subscription/) states that Canva does not offer lifetime plans, and that plans bought from unofficial channels can lead to downgrade, suspension or loss of designs. That single fact kills most "lifetime Canva for a couple of hundred rupees" ads you see in Facebook groups.

## Red flags before you pay

Pressure, secrecy and impossible offers are the strongest signals that you are dealing with a fake AI subscription seller.

Watch for these warning signs:

1. **Urgency with no details.** "Last 2 slots, pay in 5 minutes" without a written plan name, duration or access type.
2. **No written refund or warranty.** A real store publishes a [refund policy](/refund-policy/) and a [delivery policy](/delivery-policy/). Chat-only promises are easy to deny later.
3. **Password requests.** For email activations and family invitations, a seller only needs your email address. Anyone who asks for your password is a red flag.
4. **Prices that ignore reality.** Official ChatGPT and CapCut plans cost far more in USD than a casual Facebook post that claims "all AI tools for a few hundred rupees, lifetime". Compare against the labelled plans on our [ChatGPT Go](/tools/chatgpt-go/) and [Canva Pro](/tools/canva-pro/) pages.
5. **Lookalike websites and ads.** Security researchers have documented polished fake AI subscription sites that imitate real brands. Always type the store URL yourself and check the domain carefully.
6. **No access-type label.** If you cannot tell whether the plan is private, shared or invitation-based, stop. Read our guide on [private vs shared vs invitation accounts](/blog/private-vs-shared-vs-invitation-ai-accounts-pakistan.html) before paying.
7. **Payment to a random personal account with no order record.** Keep screenshots. A trustworthy seller confirms the amount, method and order on WhatsApp before you pay.

## How do fake sellers trick Pakistani buyers?

Most local scams travel through Facebook groups, Instagram DMs and random WhatsApp numbers, not through a documented storefront.

Typical tricks:

- **Screenshot theatre.** They send a screenshot of someone else's paid dashboard and claim it will be yours after payment.
- **Wrong network USDT.** They push you to send USDT, then blame you for using BEP20 when they expected TRC20 (or the reverse), and refuse to help. Always confirm the network in writing before sending.
- **Shared sold as private.** You pay for privacy, then discover other people are already logged in and can read your chats.
- **Stolen payment details.** A "checkout page" that is not the store you intended can harvest EasyPaisa OTPs or card data.
- **Post-payment silence.** After JazzCash or EasyPaisa clears, the number goes offline.

If something already went wrong, our [FAQ](/frequently-asked-questions/) explains how warranty and refunds work when a real store cannot deliver. Fake sellers rarely offer that path.

## Checklist: buy AI tools safely in Pakistan

Use this list every time, even if the price looks good.

1. Open the seller's website yourself. Check for a real catalog, policies and contact details.
2. Read the plan label: duration, price in PKR, and access type (private, shared, invitation, or own email).
3. Ask on WhatsApp: "Is this shared, private, or activated on my email?" Save the reply.
4. Confirm you will never be asked for your email password.
5. Confirm payment method (EasyPaisa, JazzCash, NayaPay, UPaisa, bank transfer or USDT) and the exact amount.
6. For USDT, confirm BEP20 or TRC20 in writing before sending.
7. Pay only after you have an order record, then send the payment screenshot.
8. Keep the chat, receipt and activation details until the plan period ends.
9. After delivery, change nothing on a shared login, and never store CNIC numbers, bank details or client contracts there.
10. If activation fails, use the written warranty path instead of arguing in a disappearing chat.

Activation at a serious store usually finishes in 5–30 minutes after payment is confirmed, with a setup guide on WhatsApp. That timeline is normal. Instant "pay and get magic lifetime access" stories are not.

## What to do if you already paid a fake seller

Act fast, document everything, and secure your accounts.

1. Stop further payments and do not send passwords or OTPs.
2. Save the WhatsApp chat, payment screenshot, account number and any links they sent.
3. If you shared an email password, change it now and turn on two-factor authentication.
4. If you joined a suspicious Canva team, copy important designs to your personal account, leave the team, and follow Canva's guidance on unofficial plans.
5. For bank or wallet disputes, contact EasyPaisa, JazzCash or your bank with the transaction ID as soon as possible.
6. Warn friends in your student or freelancer groups so the same number does not catch them next.

Buying from a documented store with PKR prices, labelled access types and a published refund policy is slower than a random DM, and that is the point. Speed favours the scammer. Process favours you.

## FAQ

### How can I spot a fake AI subscription seller quickly?

If the seller hides the access type, asks for your password, pushes a lifetime Canva deal, or refuses to share a refund policy before payment, treat them as fake and walk away.

### Kya lifetime Canva Pro real hota hai?

No. Canva states it does not offer lifetime plans. Cheap "lifetime Pro" ads are a classic scam signal, not a bargain.

### Is every cheap ChatGPT seller a scam?

No. Shared or promotional plans can be cheaper than official USD pricing and still be delivered honestly. The difference is labelling, warranty and whether the seller stays reachable after payment.

### Should I give my Gmail password for Gemini or Canva activation?

Never. A proper email activation or family invitation only needs your email address. Password requests are a hard stop.

### What payment proof should I keep?

Keep the payment screenshot, transaction ID, WhatsApp order confirmation and the final login or invitation details until the plan ends. That package is what makes warranty claims possible.

### USDT bhejne se pehle kya check karun?

Confirm the exact USDT amount and the network (BEP20 or TRC20) in writing. Coins sent on the wrong network usually cannot be recovered, and fake sellers often use that confusion as an excuse.
