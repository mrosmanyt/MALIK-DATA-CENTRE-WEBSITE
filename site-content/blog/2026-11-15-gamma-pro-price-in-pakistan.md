Gamma's help article on [exporting](https://help.gamma.app/en/articles/8022861-what-s-the-easiest-way-to-export-my-gamma) is the one to read before a lecture:

- Export formats include PDF, PNG and PowerPoint (PPTX). Google Slides is reached by uploading the PPTX.
- Tables can export as editable PowerPoint tables. Rounded table corners become square corners, because PowerPoint does not support that shape.
- Fonts are embedded in the PPTX, including custom fonts you uploaded, so the classroom PC does not need the font installed.
- Gradients and some visual effects can fall back to a simpler look in PowerPoint and PDF. Check the exported file, not only the Gamma preview.
- The "Made with Gamma" badge is stamped on PDF and PPTX exports when the workspace is on the Free plan at the moment you export. A deck you made while paid still gets the badge later if the subscription has expired. Resubscribing removes the badge from future exports. Old files you already exported keep the badge they had.

That last point is why the end date matters. If your lecture is on the final day of a one-month plan, export the PPTX while the plan is still active, and keep a copy. Do not wait until the next morning.

## How to build a lecture or a pitch that survives export

Gamma is fast at a first draft and careless if you present the first draft.

1. Write the outline in six lines before you open Gamma: who the room is, the one point they must remember, and the proof. A prompt without an outline produces a pretty deck that argues nothing.
2. Generate the deck. Then delete every slide that repeats the title in different words. Lectures fail when they are long, not when the theme is plain.
3. Replace any number the model invented. Prices, dates, citation counts and "studies show" lines are the usual errors. If you did not bring the number, take it off the slide.
4. Export to PPTX while the plan is active. Open the file in PowerPoint or in Google Slides. Click through it on the projector resolution you will actually use. A card that looks good in Gamma can overflow in PowerPoint.
5. Save the PPTX to your own drive. The private Gamma account holds the editable version. Your USB or Drive copy is what you teach from if the login is inconvenient in a computer lab.
6. For a live class, also export a PDF. Projectors and borrowed laptops open PDF more reliably than they open a PPTX with embedded fonts.

For a pitch, do the same, then add the slide an investor will ask for: who pays, how much, and what you have already done. Gamma will invent a confident slide if you leave a hole. Fill the hole with your real figures.
