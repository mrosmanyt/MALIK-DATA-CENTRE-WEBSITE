## What 200 credits roughly cover

Treat 200 credits as a small production batch.

A talking-avatar video spends credits when you generate it. A short product intro spends fewer credits than a long lesson, and a heavier model spends more than a lighter one. Because HeyGen will not lock a single rate, the honest planning method is:

1. Write the scripts you actually need this month. Count them.
2. In HeyGen, set the length and the model you intend to use, and read the credit estimate on one script.
3. Multiply that estimate by the number of videos. If the total is near 200, you have almost no spare for a retake. If it is well under 200, the pack fits. If it is over 200, you want the 1-month Creator account, or fewer videos.

Roughly, that means 200 credits fits a shop that needs a handful of presenter ads, or a teacher recording a few lesson intros, or a founder who wants one language plus a short second version. It does not fit a channel that posts a new avatar video every day. Daily publishing belongs on a plan whose credit line is the monthly Creator allotment, and you should confirm that allotment on the account before you schedule the month.

Credits on HeyGen's own monthly plan roll for one extra month only while the subscription stays active. A one-time 200-credit pack is a balance, not a promise that unused credits renew. Spend them while the account is in its paid window, and do not leave the last 40 credits for "later" if the plan end date is this week.

Retakes are the usual surprise. A mispronounced product name, a wrong price on screen, or a sentence you want shorter will spend credits again. Write the script in short lines, say the brand name the way you want it said, and render a few seconds first. Fix the script before you spend the rest of the pack on a full minute.

## Likeness, voice and what not to upload

HeyGen can build an avatar from a real person and can clone a voice. Use that only for yourself, or for someone who has agreed in writing.

- Your own face and your own voice are the normal case for a course or a shop.
- A client who wants their face in the ad should know you are generating it, and you should keep their permission with the project files.
- Do not upload a photo or a voice of a person who has not agreed. A realistic avatar of someone else is not a shortcut. It is their likeness.
- Do not use a cloned voice to imitate a bank, a teacher or a public figure. The tool will not check that for you.

Read the script out loud once before you generate. Avatar tools pronounce brand names and rupee amounts badly when the spelling is ambiguous. Write "5,800 rupees" as words if the number matters.

## A simple first video

Use this sequence so the first paid render is not a guess.

2. Pick a ready-made presenter that matches the product, or your own avatar if you already recorded the consent footage HeyGen asks for.
