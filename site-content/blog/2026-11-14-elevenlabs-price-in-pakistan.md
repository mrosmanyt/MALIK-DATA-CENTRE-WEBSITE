## How do you turn a script into a voiceover?

This is the workflow that fits a weekly video, not a studio manual.

1. Write the script in short lines. One sentence per line is easier to re-roll than a huge paragraph.
2. Sign in to the private account and open Text to Speech. Pick a stock voice first. Do not start with cloning.
3. Generate one paragraph. If the accent or pace is wrong, change the voice or the punctuation before you generate the whole script.
4. Download the audio. WAV or high-quality MP3 is the usual choice for editing.
5. Put the file on a track in [CapCut Pro](/tools/capcut-pro/) or your editor. Cut breaths and bad takes. Add music underneath at a low volume.
6. Export once. Re-exporting the same generation does not spend credits. A new generation does.

For a 10-minute narration you might be looking at roughly 8,000 to 12,000 characters, depending on how fast you write. At about one credit per character on the multilingual model, that is a small slice of a 121,000-credit official Creator pool, as long as you do not regenerate every line ten times. Dubbing and music spend much faster. ElevenLabs lists dubbing in the thousands of credits per minute. Do not dub a whole film on a Creator pool and expect it to last the month.

## Voice cloning, consent and commercial use

Professional voice cloning is the feature people search for, and it is the one that gets accounts in trouble when it is abused.

Clone a voice only when you have the right to do it. That means your own voice, or a speaker who has agreed in a way you can show later. Do not clone a celebrity, a client, a teacher or a random clip from YouTube. ElevenLabs' commercial licence covers the audio you generate under the plan rules. It does not give you the right to impersonate someone.

A sensible order:

- Use a stock voice for the first videos until the channel has a style.
- Clone your own voice when you want every video to sound like you but you do not want to record.
- Keep the original sample and the consent message with your project files.
- If a client asks you to clone their voice, get the request in writing before you upload the sample.

Our account is private, so the cloned voice sits on that login. If the plan ends and you do not control the login forever, export the audio you need and do not leave client voice models as the only copy.
