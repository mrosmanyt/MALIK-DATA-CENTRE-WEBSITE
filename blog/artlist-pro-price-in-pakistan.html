What the licence line actually covers

The tool page describes Artlist as royalty-free music, sound effects and stock footage for video. The FAQ on that page says Artlist music is licensed for YouTube, and that you clear a claim with your Artlist licence if one appears. That is the claim process, not a promise that a claim will never show up. A claim can still appear. The licence is how you answer it. Keep the licence where you can open it when YouTube's email arrives. A password you no longer have is not a licence file.

Practical rules that match that line:

  • Download the track while you can still sign in. A ready-made account is not your archive. Copy the audio file to your own drive the same day.
  • Keep the project name with the file. "YouTube video" is not a project name. "Karachi kitchen reel, November 2026" is.
  • One track in a client ad is a client job. Do not hand the same download to a second client and call it covered. Download again for the second project while you still have access, and keep a second note.
  • Sound effects and footage follow the same idea as the music. A whoosh under a title and a clip of a street still need the file on your disk.
  • Do not upload the login to a group of editors. Someone else can change the password, and then you cannot open the licence page when a claim arrives.

The family page does not print a download limit. Do not treat "no number on our page" as "unlimited forever." If Artlist's own screen shows a cap or a tier you were not expecting, screenshot it before you build a series around that track.
