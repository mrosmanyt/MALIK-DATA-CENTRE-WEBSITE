What edX Premium includes

edX Premium opens the platform's course catalog for twelve months: university-level courses in computer science, data science, business, engineering, languages, humanities and more, taught by professors from Harvard, MIT and other leading universities. The courses are structured like real university modules — lectures, readings, assignments and assessments — which is the difference between edX and a video library: you are not just watching, you are studying, and the structure keeps you honest.

The library is strongest where a degree helps: computer science fundamentals, data analysis, business and management, and the foundational subjects — maths, statistics, economics — that make every other skill easier to learn. A student preparing for a degree, a graduate filling a gap in their education, or a professional who wants the theory behind the tools they already use will all find the year useful.

Who should buy it

Buy the year if you are a student. That is the short answer. A university student in Pakistan gets twelve months of Harvard and MIT courses for Rs 400 — less than a single textbook — and the courses map directly onto the subjects already on the timetable: programming, data, business, maths. Our university students roundup puts edX in the starter stack for exactly that reason, alongside ChatGPT Go at Rs 2,200 for 6 months for the daily study help.

Buy it too if you are changing careers or filling a gap. A seller who wants to understand data, a teacher who wants a formal grounding in their subject, a professional preparing for a certification — a year is long enough to finish several real courses properly, and Rs 400 is cheap enough that finishing even one of them is value for money. Do not buy it as a vague "someday" subscription either: a year is long, but the value is in the plan, and the plan is one course at a time, finished properly.

How to get the most from a year

First, pick the course that matches what you are already doing — the subject on your timetable this semester, or the skill your next job needs — and finish it before starting another. University courses are longer than Udemy's, and the finish is the point. Second, do the assignments, not just the lectures: edX courses are built around them, and the learning happens in the doing. Third, use the year to go deep on one subject rather than shallow on ten; three finished courses in data science beat ten half-watched introductions.

Fourth, pair the learning with the tools that make study faster. ChatGPT Go at Rs 2,200 for 6 months explains the hard lecture in plain language when you get stuck, and Gemini Pro at Rs 1,500 a year summarises readings and builds revision notes. The course teaches you the subject; the AI tools make the studying faster. Together, the year costs less than Rs 2,000 — and that is a full study system, not a single course.

edX vs Udemy: which one first

edX and Udemy answer different questions. edX is university learning: structured, theoretical, taught by professors, and best for foundations — the computer science, the maths, the business theory. Udemy is practical learning: shorter, skill-first, taught by practitioners, and best for doing — the Python project, the design tool, the marketing campaign. If you need the foundation, buy edX first at Rs 400 for the year; if you need the skill this month, buy Udemy Personal at Rs 1,250 for the month. Most serious learners end up with both, and together they cost Rs 1,650 — a complete learning system for less than most single subscriptions.
