Shared plan vs dedicated slot

The Rs 2,500 shared plan and the Rs 26,000 slot are both called Google AI Ultra, and they are not the same product. The shared plan is a shared login: cheap, fast to start, and fine for learning, testing prompts and making a few videos. Its limits are the shared account's limits, and other users are on the same login. The 25k slot is yours alone for the month: 25,000 credits, a dedicated slot, and a 28-day warranty. If you are producing client videos every day, the slot is the honest choice — the shared plan will frustrate you the week a deadline lands. If you are a student, a hobbyist or an editor deciding whether Veo is worth it at all, the shared plan is the honest choice — Rs 2,500 to find out beats Rs 26,000 to find out.

The Flow Ultra 20x row at Rs 29,999 sits between them in spirit: it is the video-first plan, delivered on WhatsApp, for buyers whose whole job is Flow and Veo 3. Pick it when video is the only thing you need Ultra for, and pick the slot when you also want the heavy Gemini Ultra usage that comes with it.

Ultra or Gemini Pro?

Most buyers asking about Ultra should first check whether Gemini Pro already does the job. Gemini Pro is Rs 1,500 for 12 months on your own Gmail — a full year, on your own email, for less than one month of the cheapest Ultra row. It covers writing, study, research, Gmail and Docs, and it includes Veo access at the lower Pro tier. Our Ultra vs Gemini Pro comparison for video creators works out the Flow credits per rupee, and the short answer is this: if your month is mostly writing, study and chat, buy Gemini Pro and keep Rs 1,000 a month in your pocket. If your month is mostly Veo video, buy Ultra — and buy the row that matches how much video you actually make, not the most expensive one.

There is also a middle step many buyers miss. Our Gemini Pro how-to guide walks through the same WhatsApp order for the Rs 1,500 year plan. Buy that first, use it for two weeks, and upgrade to Ultra only when you hit its video limits. That is a cheaper experiment than starting on Ultra and discovering you only needed Pro.

Mistakes to avoid

First, buying the most expensive row first. The 25k slot is a production tool; a first-time buyer learns nothing from it that the Rs 2,500 shared plan cannot teach. Second, paying before confirming the plan and price on WhatsApp — the catalog price and the price on the day can differ, and the confirmation message is your record. Third, treating a shared login like a private account: never store client files, passwords or unpublished work on it, and never change its password. Fourth, forgetting the warranty window: the shared plan's 25-day warranty and the slot's 28-day warranty are counted from activation, so test everything in the first day, not the last. Fifth, ordering from a seller who asks for your Google password or an OTP — that is the single clearest sign of a scam in this market.
