Dictate, then edit. Do not skip the read

The product turns speech into text and formats it. It does not know which sentence you meant to keep. Read the paragraph before it goes to a client, a lecturer or a GitHub comment.

Use it for:

  • A first draft of an email or a document you will edit.
  • Notes after a call, if you are allowed to record or dictate that call. The tool is not permission to record someone who has not agreed.
  • Prompts and comments while you code, if you already have the editor open.

Do not use the first dictated draft as the final text when a wrong word would cost money. Prices, names, medicine, and legal lines need a human read. Filler-word removal can also drop a word you meant. The spec says smart formatting. It does not say the paragraph is checked.

Grammarly Pro is the tool for text that already exists. The 1-month plan is Rs 1,400, activated on your email. The 12-month plan is Rs 3,500, with a listed original price of Rs 12,000, also on your email, and the specs include a 1-year full warranty. Grammarly checks grammar, tone and clarity. It does not listen to you speak. Wispr does not replace that check. A dictated email can still go through Grammarly if the English has to be clean. Buying both is reasonable for someone who speaks the draft and then submits it. Buying both on day one, before the free Wispr test, is how you pay Rs 7,500 plus Rs 3,500 for a workflow you have not tried.

Devices and language

The tool page names Mac, Windows and iPhone. It does not name an Android phone in that sentence. If you dictate on Android, ask before you pay. A year on the wrong device is a long time to discover the app you wanted is not the one we listed.

Language is the other gate. The page says many languages, and it tells you to test the free version with your language first. Urdu, Punjabi, or a mix of Urdu and English in one sentence may not behave like a clean English dictation. We will not claim a language works because a buyer hopes it does. Run the free test on the same kind of sentence you will use at work. If names and code words fail, decide whether you can correct them fast enough to justify the year.

A headset or a quiet room changes the result more than the plan name. Pro does not fix a microphone that points at a ceiling fan. Test in the room you will actually use.
