What Ideogram Plus is

Ideogram is an AI image generator, and Plus is its paid plan. You type a description — a poster, a logo, a product shot, a book cover — and it generates the image. What sets it apart from other generators is text: where most image models garble words, Ideogram renders them cleanly, in the right spelling and in a style that matches the image. That single strength decides who should buy it and who should not.

It is not a design suite and not a photo editor. If you need templates, brand kits and a full editor, Canva Pro at Rs 400 for 12 months is the better buy. If you need AI video, look at Veo 3 through Google Flow or Higgsfield. Ideogram does one job — images with correct text — and does it better than the general tools.

What it is good at

  • Posters and banners: sale posters, event banners and shop boards where the headline has to read correctly.
  • Thumbnails: YouTube thumbnails with a short title baked into the image, in a style that matches the video.
  • Logos and wordmarks: first drafts of a brand name rendered as a logo, to shortlist before a designer polishes it.
  • Worksheets and cards: teachers and small businesses use it for labelled diagrams, greeting cards and certificates where the text matters as much as the picture.
  • Product mockups: packaging and labels with the product name printed on them, for a shop listing or a pitch deck.

The common thread is text you can read. If your image has no words in it, almost any generator will do. If the words are the point, Ideogram is the tool.

Who should buy it

Buy Ideogram Plus if you make images with text every week: a shop owner posting sale graphics, a YouTuber making thumbnails, a teacher making worksheets, or a freelancer delivering social media posts for clients. At Rs 2,400 it pays for itself the first time a client approves a poster without asking for the spelling to be fixed.

And do not buy it as a video tool: it makes still images, not video. If your work is video, the money belongs in Gemini Pro for Veo access instead.

How to write a good Ideogram prompt

Ideogram rewards the same habit as every image tool: say what the image is, say what the text says, and say the style. Put the exact words in quotes inside the prompt — for example, a poster "with the text 'Grand Opening Sale' in bold white letters" — and name the layout: "centred headline, shop photo below, red and white colours". Short prompts work better than paragraphs: one sentence for the image, one for the text, one for the style.

Generate two or three versions of the same prompt and pick the cleanest text, because even the best text model slips on a long headline. If a word comes out wrong, shorten the headline before you regenerate — five words render more reliably than fifteen. And keep the prompts you like: a poster that worked once is a template you can reuse for the next sale by changing only the words in quotes.

Ideogram vs Canva Pro and Higgsfield

The three tools sit in different jobs, and the prices show it. Canva Pro is Rs 400 for 12 months and is the design suite: templates, text tools and an editor, with AI images as one feature inside it. Ideogram Plus at Rs 2,400 is the specialist: better text-in-image than Canva's generator, but no templates and no editor. Higgsfield starts from Rs 6,400 and is the video tool, for creators who want camera-control AI video. A shop that posts daily graphics usually ends up with Canva Pro for the layout and Ideogram Plus for the hero image with the headline on it — the two complement each other rather than compete.
