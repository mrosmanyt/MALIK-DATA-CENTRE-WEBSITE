What does official Cursor charge in 2026?

Cursor's own pricing changed. Do not assume a blog from 2025 still matches the checkout page.

Cursor's current help page on pricing and plans says new customers start on what Cursor calls The X Subscription. The public individual prices, excluding tax, are:

  • Plus: $8 a month, or $80 a year
  • Premium: $30 a month, or $300 a year
  • Super: $100 a month, or $1,000 a year
  • Ultra: $200 a month, or $2,000 a year

The same page lists the older Pro plan at $20 a month as a legacy plan. Legacy plans keep working for people already on them. New customers are not sold Pro. Cursor says Premium is the closest current tier to legacy Pro, not an identical copy of it.

On current paid plans, Cursor's models and pricing docs say each tier includes a pool of usage for Cursor's own models, shared across the editor agent and related Grok products. That pool refreshes weekly. Third-party models on Premium and above are billed separately, at the model provider's list price, from prepaid credit. They are not included in the $30.

Legacy Pro, described on Cursor's legacy plans page, splits usage into two monthly pools and still includes unlimited Tab completions. Extra use can be turned on as on-demand billing. Unused included usage does not roll over.

That is why a rupee price and a dollar price are hard to line up. Our store sells a private account labelled Cursor Pro. Cursor's public site may show Plus, Premium, Super or Ultra on a new signup. Before you order, message us and ask which tier will be on the account that day. The live plans and the access type are on our Cursor Pro page.

What do you get in the editor?

Cursor is a desktop code editor built on VS Code. Your extensions, themes and most keyboard shortcuts carry over. The paid plan is there to raise how much AI help you can use while you work.

From the way we describe the plan, and from Cursor's own docs, the useful pieces are:

  • Tab autocomplete. Ghost text as you type. On the legacy Pro plans this is unlimited.
  • Agent and chat on your codebase. You can ask about the project you have open, not only a snippet you paste.
  • Frontier coding models, with a usage pool. Heavy agent runs spend the pool faster than short completions. Watch the usage meter in the editor.
  • A private account. Your projects and chat history stay on that account, not on a shared login.

A few things the plan does not do:

  • It does not host your app. You still deploy wherever you already deploy.
  • It does not include unlimited calls to every third-party model. Cursor's current docs bill those separately once you leave the included pool.
  • It is not a substitute for reading the diff. The agent will happily edit the wrong file if the instruction is vague.

If your catalog description somewhere still says "unlimited Claude" or a specific model name from last year, treat that as outdated marketing. Model names move. The constraint that matters is the usage pool on the account you receive.

Choose Cursor if you already have a codebase, use Git, and want AI inside a desktop editor. Choose Replit Core if you want the editor, the agent and the deployment in the browser, including from a phone or a lab PC. Choose Lovable Pro if you are a founder or freelancer who needs a working web app and you do not want to live in a code editor. Many people sketch the first version in Lovable and move the repo to Cursor once it grows. You do not need all three on day one.
