The yearly plan, the $100 credits, and one month

The 12-month listing is a private account. The description says you write, build and deploy in a collaborative cloud IDE, with the Replit AI assistant. The specs name autocomplete and chat, boosted workspace VM resources, 1-click cloud deployments, and a 1-year warranty. Your projects sit on that private account. This is the row for a student who will still be in the IDE in month six, and for a freelancer who deploys small apps from the browser.

The $100 listing is a ready-made account pre-loaded with $100 of top-up credits. The specs call it a ready-made private account, with the AI assistant, fast VMs, and a 20-day warranty. Buy this when the work is Agent-heavy and you want a lump of credits on day one, rather than a 12-month seat. When the loaded balance is spent, the account does not silently refill itself at our price. Ask us before you assume a second $100 is included.

The 1-month listing is Replit Core on a ready-made account, shorter than the year, with a 3-day warranty and setup help on WhatsApp. Use it to learn the IDE, finish a class assignment, or see whether you prefer the browser to a desktop editor. It is a weak fit if you already know you will code here all year.

A practical split:

  1. You are learning, or you switch between a lab PC and a phone. Start with the month at Rs 1,850, or go straight to the year if the course runs past a month.
  2. You want the Agent to do a lot of building in a short window. The $100 credit account at Rs 9,500 is the credit lump.
  3. You want the IDE, deployments and the assistant for 12 months on a private account. That is Rs 10,700.

Replit runs in the browser. You can open it on a machine where you cannot install software. The Agent can draft and edit the project that lives on Replit, and the yearly listing includes 1-click deployments. You still read the diff. A confident Agent edit can break a working app.

What Replit lists, and why $100 here is not Replit Pro

Replit's pricing page is the public card. When we checked it, Core showed $20 struck through and $18 a month when billed annually (official). The next card on that page is the higher commercial plan, shown at $100 struck through and $90 a month when billed annually (official). Replit's help docs say Core is $204 a year (official), and that Core includes $20 in monthly credits (official) which reset each cycle and do not roll over. Those are Replit's prices. Ours are the rupee rows above.

The phrase "$100" appears in two different products, and mixing them up is expensive.

  • Our Rs 9,500 row is a ready-made Core account with $100 of credits loaded, and a 20-day warranty line.
  • Replit's own higher plan is priced from $100 a month (official) on the pricing page. We do not sell that plan under a separate name. Do not order the Rs 9,500 row expecting the commercial tier, the longer database rollback, or a monthly $100 grant that renews.

Core's monthly credits, on Replit's docs, are a small allowance that resets. Our yearly row is a 12-month private account at Rs 10,700. It is not a promise that Replit will add $20 of fresh credits every month on top of a number we did not list. After activation, open the billing page and read the credit balance that is actually there. The $100 figure belongs only to the Rs 9,500 listing.

Agent use spends credits as the task gets longer. Replit's own pages describe that spend, and they warn that the Agent can be wrong. Set a budget in the Replit account if the product offers one, so a long run cannot keep billing past the credits you meant to use. Our price is the account. It is not an open tab at Replit.

Who should not buy Cursor instead

Cursor and Replit both involve AI and code. They are different workplaces. The side-by-side is on Cursor vs Replit vs Lovable, and the rupee table for the editor is the Cursor Pro price guide.

Cursor Pro on our store is Rs 4,200 for 1 month or Rs 21,000 for 12 months, both on a private account. The monthly listing shows an original price of Rs 8,000. The yearly listing shows an original price of Rs 56,000. Cursor is a desktop editor for a repository on your machine. It does not host the app for you.

Buy Cursor, not the Replit year, when all of these are true:

  • You already work in VS Code or you are willing to install Cursor on your own computer.
  • The code lives in a repo you control, and you want the agent to edit that repo.
  • You deploy somewhere else, on a host you already use.

Do not buy Cursor when any of these are true:

  • The computer is a lab machine, a shared family PC, or a phone, and you cannot install a desktop editor. Replit is the browser IDE. The 1-month account at Rs 1,850 is the test.
  • You want the app published from the same place you built it. The yearly Replit listing includes 1-click deployments. Cursor does not replace a host.
  • You do not have a codebase yet, and you want a site from a plain-English prompt. That job is Lovable, on the comparison page, not a reason to buy either IDE out of habit.
  • You only needed a month of browser coding. Cursor's year at Rs 21,000 is the wrong size for a one-week class. Replit's month is the smaller commitment. Replit's year at Rs 10,700 is the smaller year if the browser is the workplace.

A freelancer who maintains a client's existing GitHub repo should buy Cursor and clone the repo. A student who submits from the Replit link their teacher already uses should buy Replit. Buying both in the same month makes sense only after you have hit a limit in one of them.
