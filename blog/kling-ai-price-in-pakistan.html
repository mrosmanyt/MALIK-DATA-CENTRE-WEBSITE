Kling AI makes short videos from a text prompt or a still image. Shops use it for a product turning on a table. Editors use it for a three-second insert they cannot film. This is the Kling AI price in Pakistan, the difference between the 660-credit plan and the 750-credit plan, and when a different video tool is the better buy. It is for people making ads and product clips, not for people who need a talking presenter.

Kling spends credits by the settings you pick. Length, resolution and audio each change the cost, and Kling's help pages move when a new model ships. This guide does not invent a "one video equals X credits" rate. The estimate in the generate button is the rate for that clip.

A practical way to spend 660 credits on ads:

  • Write five prompts, not fifty. One product, one motion, one background.
  • Generate the shortest length that shows the product. A three-second or five-second clip is an ad insert. A ten-second clip can cost about twice the credits if the meter is per second.
  • Turn audio off if you will add your own voice or music later. Native audio is convenient and it is often the setting that doubles the spend.
  • Keep one generation as a spare. The first result often has a warped label or a hand that looks wrong. Product text inside the frame is the usual failure. Put the price and the brand name on the clip afterwards in an editor.

750 credits is 90 credits more than 660. That is spare for a few extra takes, or for one longer clip, not a different kind of software. Buy 750 when you already know 660 runs out before the ads are approved. Buy 660 when you are testing whether Kling belongs in the workflow at all. The 660 plan is the lower price, at Rs 1,300 against Rs 2,400.

Text-to-video for a product clip

Kling is a generator. You describe a shot, or you upload a photo and describe the motion. It is a good fit when filming is awkward: a bottle on a marble surface, a fabric fold, a shoe rotating, a steam shot over a cup. It is a bad fit when the client needs the real product, the real shop and a real person. Generate the insert. Film the proof.

Use prompts that a camera operator would understand.

  • Name the subject, the motion and the lens feel. "A glass perfume bottle rotates slowly on a dark stone surface, soft side light, no text."
  • Say what must not happen. "No hands. No extra logos. Label stays sharp."
  • Keep the first test at the lower length. If the shape holds, render one longer version.
  • Export and watch it on a phone. A clip that looks fine on a laptop can shimmer on a small screen.

Image-to-video is the safer path for a real product. Upload a photo you took, then ask for a small motion: a push-in, a turn, light moving across the surface. You are less likely to get a different bottle than the one you sell. Text-to-video is better when you do not have the photo yet and you need a concept to show a client.

Our listing describes high-definition output and, on the 660 plan, fast-queue processing. Queues and model names change. If a generation sits for a long time, screenshot it. Do not start ten more jobs while the first one is stuck, or you will spend the credits twice when they all finish.

Higgsfield is the studio. The Plus listing is 1,200 credits at Rs 7,500, with an original price of Rs 15,000 and a 20-day warranty on the specs. The Pro listing is 610 credits at Rs 6,400. Pro is not "Plus with more credits". It is a smaller credit pack at a lower price. Pick Higgsfield when you want camera moves and more than one model. Pick Kling when you already know you want Kling's own text-to-video and you want the lower entry price.

A presenter who looks at the lens and says the offer is a different product. Kling will invent a person if you ask, and that person will not be a stable spokesperson you can reuse next week. For a face that repeats, use a talking-avatar tool and keep Kling for the cutaways.

After the clip exists, you still edit. Captions, a logo and a price sticker belong in CapCut Pro. Generating inside Kling and posting the raw file is how product text ends up misspelled.

A one-evening test before you buy the larger pack

If you have not used Kling before, buy the Rs 1,300 plan and spend one evening on a single product.

  1. Take three photos of the product: front, three-quarter, and a detail of the material.
  2. Write one motion prompt per photo. Keep each clip short.
  3. Generate once. If the label bends, fix the prompt and generate once more. Stop after two tries on the same photo.
  4. Drop the best clip under a real voice or a caption in CapCut.
  5. Show it to one person who does not know you used a generator. If they ask "is that our product?", the tool is useful. If they ask "what is that?", another 90 credits will not fix the idea.

That evening tells you whether 660 is enough for the month or whether the 750-credit row is the one to renew into. It also tells you whether the job was never a generation job and you should film the product instead.
