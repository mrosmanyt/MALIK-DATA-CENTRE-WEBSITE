Content writers in Pakistan — bloggers, copywriters, SEO writers, agency writers — live inside AI tools now. A draft starts in a chatbot, gets polished in a grammar checker, and gets checked for originality before it goes to the client. The question is which tools to pay for, because five subscriptions add up fast. This guide builds the writer's stack from tools actually in the MALIK DATA CENTRE catalog, with real PKR prices, so you can see the monthly cost before you spend it.

The writer's stack at a glance

Five tools cover the whole writing job, and no single one does it all. ChatGPT Go drafts, Claude API handles long-form reasoning and research-heavy writing, Grammarly Pro polishes, QuillBot Premium rewrites and paraphrases, and Gemini Pro backs it all up inside Google Docs and Gmail.

The cheapest useful core is ChatGPT Go plus Grammarly Pro: about Rs 367 a month for Go (Rs 2,200 spread over six months) plus Rs 1,400 for a month of Grammarly, which is Rs 1,767 for the first month and less after that if you switch Grammarly to the yearly plan. Add QuillBot at Rs 1,800 a month only when paraphrasing is a daily need, not a sometimes need — many writers find Grammarly's rewrites enough and never need both.

ChatGPT Go: the drafting engine

ChatGPT Go at Rs 2,200 for 6 months is the cheapest way into ChatGPT's paid tier, and for a writer it is the workhorse: outlines, first drafts, headlines, meta descriptions, social captions, client emails. It gives you longer chats, file uploads and image generation over the free version, which matters when a brief arrives as a PDF and the draft needs to reference it. Our ChatGPT Go price guide covers exactly what Go includes.

One honest note: Go is a shared premium account, not your own private login. Keep client-confidential material out of it, and read our guide to private vs shared AI accounts before you buy any shared plan. For general drafting it is fine; for sensitive client work, it is not.

Claude API: the long-form brain

When a piece is long — a 3,000-word guide, a research-backed article, a white paper — ChatGPT Go starts to feel thin, and that is where Claude comes in. Claude API credits are price on request, quoted on WhatsApp for the pack you need, and they buy access to Claude models through an API key for your own workflow. Writers use it for research synthesis, structuring long pieces, and rewriting drafts with more care than a chatbot gives.

This is a power tool, not a starter tool. If you are writing short blog posts and social captions, Go plus Grammarly is enough and Claude API is overkill. If you write long-form for clients who pay per word, Claude's output quality pays for the credits. Start with a small pack — the 10M token 1-day pack — and measure how much a real article costs you before buying bigger.

Grammarly Pro: the polish

Grammarly Pro costs Rs 1,400 for 1 month or Rs 3,500 for 12 months, activated on your own email. It is the only tool in this stack whose whole job is the final 10%: grammar, punctuation, tone, clarity, and a plagiarism check before you send the draft to a client. For a writer, that last check is the difference between a draft and a deliverable — clients notice clean copy, and they notice errors faster.

The yearly plan is the smart buy if you write every day: Rs 3,500 for 12 months is about Rs 292 a month, against Rs 1,400 for a single month. Buy one month first to confirm it fits your workflow, then switch to the year when you are sure.

QuillBot Premium: the rewriter

QuillBot Premium costs Rs 1,800 for 1 month on a ready-made account. Its job is paraphrasing: take a sentence, a paragraph or a draft and rewrite it in different words without changing the meaning. Writers use it to fix repetition, to rephrase a client's rough notes into clean copy, and to summarise long source material into something usable.

QuillBot overlaps with Grammarly — both rewrite — so most writers should pick one, not both. Pick QuillBot if paraphrasing and summarising are daily tasks. Pick Grammarly if grammar, tone and the plagiarism check matter more. Our StealthGPT vs QuillBot comparison is worth reading if rewriting is your main need, because it covers the detection angle too.

Gemini Pro: the Google-side backup

Gemini Pro costs Rs 1,500 for 12 months on your own Gmail, with the stronger Gemini model, 2 TB of storage, and Gemini built into Gmail, Docs and Sheets. For a writer whose drafts live in Google Docs, that integration is the point: summarise a document, draft inside Docs, and research from Gmail without switching apps. At about Rs 125 a month it is the cheapest full plan in this stack, and it pairs naturally with ChatGPT Go — one for drafting, one for the Google workflow.

A writer's daily workflow with the stack

Here is how the five tools fit a normal working day. The brief arrives by email: Gemini Pro summarises it inside Gmail. The outline and first draft come from ChatGPT Go. A long or research-heavy section goes through Claude API for a careful pass. The draft gets rewritten where it repeats itself — QuillBot, if you have it — and then Grammarly Pro runs the final check for grammar, tone and plagiarism before delivery. Nothing in that chain is wasted, and nothing is duplicated: each tool does the job the others do worst.

The monthly cost of the full stack, if you buy everything monthly, is about Rs 5,400 a month — but nobody should start there. Start with ChatGPT Go (Rs 2,200 for six months) and Grammarly Pro (Rs 1,400 for a month), add Gemini Pro (Rs 1,500 for a year) when the Google integration starts to matter, and add QuillBot or Claude API only when a specific job demands them. A stack you actually use beats a stack you pay for.

Which AI tool is best for writing blog posts?

ChatGPT Go is the best starting point for blog posts — Rs 2,200 for 6 months covers outlines, drafts, headlines and meta descriptions. Add Grammarly Pro for the final polish and plagiarism check before publishing.

Do I need both Grammarly and QuillBot?

Usually not. Grammarly is stronger for grammar, tone and plagiarism checks; QuillBot is stronger for paraphrasing and summarising. Pick the one that matches your daily work — most writers pick Grammarly first.

Is ChatGPT Go enough for professional writing?

For most client work, yes — with Grammarly Pro on top for the final check. For long-form, research-heavy pieces, add Claude API credits. Go is a shared premium account, so keep client-confidential material out of it.
