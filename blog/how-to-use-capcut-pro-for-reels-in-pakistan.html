How to Use CapCut Pro for Reels in Pakistan

Most Reels, TikToks and Shorts in Pakistan are edited in CapCut, and most of them are edited badly — not because the editor is weak, but because the workflow is missing. A good Reel is not a long video cut short; it is a short video built for the first three seconds, with captions, music and a clean export. This guide is the beginner workflow for CapCut Pro in Pakistan: what to do in which order, which settings to use, and when paying for Pro actually changes the result.

What you need before the first edit

You need CapCut on your phone or PC — the app is free to install, and Pro is an upgrade inside it, not a separate app. You need your clips shot or saved in vertical 9:16, because Reels are vertical and a horizontal clip cropped later always looks wrong. And you need a rough idea of the Reel before you open the timeline: one idea, one hook, one call to action. A Reel that tries to say three things says none of them.

Start on the free version. Learn the cut, the captions and the export first — those three skills are the whole job, and Pro does not do them for you. Pay for Pro when you can name the specific Pro feature you need, which is usually a premium effect, a template, or a caption style the free version locks. Our CapCut Pro review covers what the upgrade changes and what it does not.

The beginner workflow, step by step

Step 1 — Import and set the canvas. Open a new project, import your clips, and confirm the canvas is 9:16 vertical. If a clip was shot horizontally, reframe it now — do not leave it for the export.

Step 2 — Cut ruthlessly. Watch each clip once and cut everything that is not the point: the pause before you speak, the repeated word, the dead second at the end. A Reel should be 15 to 45 seconds for most topics, and every second has to earn its place. Cut on the beat of your voice, not on a timer.

Step 3 — Put the hook first. The first three seconds decide whether anyone watches. Move your strongest line, your most surprising visual, or your clearest promise to the very start. "How to buy Gemini Pro in Pakistan for Rs 1,500" beats "Hello friends, welcome to my video" every time.

Step 4 — Add captions. Use auto-captions to generate the text, then fix the mistakes by hand — auto-captions mishear Urdu names, brand names and numbers. Keep captions short, one idea per line, and large enough to read on a phone without fullscreen. This step is covered in detail below because it matters more than any effect.

Step 5 — Music and sound. Add music under the voice, not over it. Pick a track that fits the mood, keep it quiet enough that the voice stays clear, and check the licensing — this is where Pro's licensed music and the free library differ, and it is covered in the music section below.

Step 6 — Effects, transitions and colour. One transition style for the whole Reel, not a different one every cut. A light colour adjustment — a little contrast, a little saturation — is enough. Effects are seasoning, not the meal.

Step 7 — Export and post. Export at 1080x1920, 30 fps, as described below, then post from the phone the Reel was made for. Check the Reel on your own feed before you share it anywhere else.

Captions and text that get watched

Most Reels are watched on mute, so captions are not decoration — they are the video for a large part of your audience. Auto-captions get you most of the way there, but they need a human pass: fix names, fix numbers, fix the line breaks so a sentence does not split in a strange place. Keep each caption line short enough to read in one glance, and use a style that matches the Reel — bold and punchy for a hook, plain and calm for an explanation.

Pro's value here is the premium caption styles and templates: animated words, highlighted keywords, designs that look made rather than default. If your Reels are for clients, those styles are the difference between a video that looks edited and a video that looks posted. For your own account, the free caption styles are fine while you are learning — spend the learning time on the cut, not the font.

Music and sound without copyright strikes

Music is where beginners lose Reels. A trending song ripped from another video can get your Reel muted, blocked, or claimed — and a muted Reel is a dead Reel. Use music from inside CapCut's own library or from Instagram's audio library, where the licence is already handled, and keep the volume under your voice so the words stay clear.

For client work, the rule is stricter: use licensed music only, and keep a record of where it came from. CapCut Pro includes access to more licensed music and sound effects than the free version, and for editors who also need music for YouTube, Artlist Pro at Rs 3,500 is the dedicated library — our video editors guide compares the music and stock options.

Export settings for Instagram Reels

Export vertical video at 1080x1920 and 30 frames per second. That is the Reels standard: sharp on phones, small enough to upload on mobile data, and exactly what Instagram expects. Higher resolutions and frame rates look no better on a phone screen and only make the upload slower and the file bigger.

Before you export, watch the whole Reel once from start to finish on the phone — not on a PC, and not in the editor. Check the captions are readable, the music is not drowning the voice, and nothing important sits under Instagram's own interface at the bottom of the screen. If it passes that test, export and post. If it does not, fix it now, because re-uploading a Reel after posting costs you the first hour of reach.

Beginner mistakes that kill reach

Posting a horizontal video. A landscape clip in a vertical feed is small, ignored and skipped. Shoot vertical or reframe before you edit.

Burying the hook. If the interesting part starts at second eight, most viewers never see it. Put it first, then explain.

No captions. A Reel without captions loses every muted viewer, and that is most of them. Caption everything, even if the audio is perfect.

Five transition styles in one Reel. Pick one and use it sparingly. Variety in transitions reads as a beginner's showreel, not a professional edit.

Exporting at the wrong size. A 720p export on a 1080p feed looks soft, and a 4K export wastes your data and your time. 1080x1920 at 30 fps, every time.
