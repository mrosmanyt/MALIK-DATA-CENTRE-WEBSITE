An e-commerce seller in Pakistan — Daraz, Shopify, WooCommerce, or a WhatsApp store — sells with pictures, words and video, in that order. The product photo decides the click, the listing decides the order, and the Reel decides whether anyone ever sees the store. Five tools in the MALIK DATA CENTRE catalog cover all three, and the starter stack costs about Rs 1,050 a month. This guide prices the stack in rupees and shows how it fits a seller's week.

The seller’s stack at a glance

Five tools cover the job. Canva Pro makes the product photos and banners, ChatGPT Go writes the listings and replies, CapCut Pro cuts the Reels, Kling AI turns photos into product videos, and HeyGen makes avatar ads that speak.

Canva Pro: the product photos

Canva Pro costs Rs 400 for 12 months, activated on your own email — the cheapest tool in this stack, and the one a seller uses every day. Product photos on clean backgrounds, discount banners, Daraz and Shopify listing images in the right sizes, WhatsApp catalogue covers, and Instagram posts: background remover, Magic Resize and the brand kit with your store's colours and logo do the daily work. Our Canva Pro activation guide walks through the setup.

For a seller, the rule is simple: one template per product type, one brand kit for the store, and every new listing takes ten minutes instead of an hour. If you buy only one tool from this whole guide, buy Canva Pro.

ChatGPT Go: the listings and replies

Listings are the part of selling nobody sees and everybody judges. ChatGPT Go at Rs 2,200 for 6 months writes them: titles with the keywords buyers search for, bullet-point descriptions, and the "what is in the box" lines that stop the "yeh kya hai?" messages. It also drafts the customer replies — the delivery questions, the size questions, the "discount milega?" messages that eat a seller's evening.

One caution: Go is a shared premium account, not a private login. Never paste a supplier's prices, your margins or a customer's address into it, and read our guide to private vs shared AI accounts before buying. For listings and replies it is fine; for anything with real numbers in it, it is not.

CapCut Pro: the Reels

CapCut Pro starts at Rs 650 for a 1-month team invite, with other options at Rs 800 for a month on two devices and Rs 1,250 for a private monthly activation. Reels are where the reach is — Instagram, TikTok and YouTube Shorts all reward short video, and a product Reel outperforms a static post by an order of magnitude. Auto-captions, trending templates, background removal and clean exports without a watermark are the Pro features that show up in a seller's ads.

Our CapCut Pro Reels workflow guide covers the actual editing process, so this guide stays on the buying decision: the Rs 650 team invite is the cheapest door in, and the Rs 1,250 private month is the one to pick when the store's account needs a private activation.

Kling AI: the product videos

A photo shows the product; a video sells it. Kling AI costs Rs 1,300 for 660 monthly credits or Rs 2,400 for a 750-credit plan, and it turns a product photo into a moving video clip — fabric that moves, a bottle that rotates, a gadget that opens. That is the clip you cut in CapCut and post as a Reel, and it is the difference between a listing that looks like everyone else's and one that stops the scroll.

Buy Kling when video ads are already part of your marketing. The 660-credit monthly plan is the starter: generate the clips for the week's bestsellers, not for the whole catalogue at once. If you are not posting video yet, start with CapCut and add Kling the month your first Reel brings an order.

HeyGen: the avatar ads

HeyGen Creator costs Rs 5,800 for a 200-credit private pack or Rs 9,150 for a 1-month Creator account. It makes talking-avatar videos from a script: an avatar that presents your product, explains the offer and asks for the order, in Urdu or English, without you filming yourself. For sellers who do not want to be on camera, that is the whole point — a presenter who never gets tired and never needs a reshoot.

HeyGen is the most expensive tool in this stack per use, so buy it last. Start with the 200-credit pack, make one avatar ad for your bestseller, and run it as a Meta ad. If the ad pays for the credits, buy more. If it does not, you have spent Rs 5,800 learning something useful instead of Rs 9,150 a month on a subscription you do not need.

A seller’s weekly workflow

Here is how the five tools fit a normal week. Monday: new products get their photos in Canva Pro and their listings from ChatGPT Go. Tuesday: the week's Reels get cut in CapCut Pro, with Kling clips for the two bestsellers. Wednesday: the avatar ad for the weekend offer gets made in HeyGen. Thursday and Friday: orders come in, and ChatGPT Go drafts the replies. The weekend is for the sale post in Canva and the Reel that pushes it. Each tool does one job, and the handoffs between them are the workflow.

Start with the three cheap ones — Canva Pro (Rs 400 a year), ChatGPT Go (Rs 2,200 for six months) and CapCut Pro (from Rs 650 a month) — and add Kling and HeyGen only when video is already bringing orders. A stack that matches the sales beats a stack that matches a wishlist.
