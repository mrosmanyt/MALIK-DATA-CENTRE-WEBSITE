Lesson planning with ChatGPT Go

Lesson planning is where most teachers lose their evenings. ChatGPT Go at Rs 2,200 for 6 months — about Rs 367 a month — is the cheapest way to get a paid ChatGPT plan in Pakistan, and it is built for exactly this kind of everyday drafting work. Give it your topic, class level and time available, and it returns a structured plan with objectives, activities and homework. Paste a textbook paragraph in and ask for five quiz questions at three difficulty levels. Draft a term outline in one sitting instead of three.

Go also handles the writing teachers do outside the classroom: notices, circulars, assembly scripts and the polite-but-firm message to a parent about homework. The full price breakdown is in our ChatGPT Go price guide, and if you want the bigger plan, the Plus vs Go comparison explains when the upgrade is worth it. Slides and worksheets with Gamma Pro

Gamma Pro turns a topic into a finished slide deck: type "photosynthesis for class 7, 10 slides" and it builds the deck, the layout and the visuals. For a teacher, that replaces the Sunday-night slide marathon with a ten-minute review and edit. It also makes worksheets and handouts in the same style, and decks export to PDF and PowerPoint, which matters when the school computer has no internet or the projector only opens PowerPoint files.

Gamma Pro is Rs 9,500 for 12 months on a private account, or Rs 2,400 for 1 month if you want to test it on a single unit first. The monthly option is the sensible start for a teacher: buy one month, build a full term's decks, and only then decide on the year. Our Gamma Pro price guide has the full breakdown.

Gemini Pro: research, marking and parent messages

Gemini Pro at Rs 1,500 for 12 months on your own Gmail is the strongest value on this list: a full year of Google's Pro-tier AI for less than most teachers spend on photocopies in a term. It sits inside Gmail, Docs and Sheets, so it can summarise a long email thread from a parent, draft a reply in your tone, and help build a marking rubric in a spreadsheet. For research, it answers with sources you can check before you teach from them — which matters when a student asks where an answer came from.

Gemini Pro also includes 2 TB of Google storage and Gemini inside the Google apps teachers already use, so lesson files, scanned worksheets and class photos stop competing for space. A teacher's weekly AI workflow

Tools only help if they fit the week. Here is a realistic one, using all four:

  • Monday: plan the week's lessons with ChatGPT Go, then build the slides in Gamma Pro from the same plan.
  • Tuesday: make the worksheet in Canva Pro, using the lesson objectives as the brief.
  • Wednesday: use Gemini Pro to check facts, find a short video or reading, and draft the parent message for the week's homework.
  • Thursday: mark with a rubric drafted in Gemini Pro, and let ChatGPT Go turn your rough comments into polite, specific feedback.
  • Friday: reuse the week's best slides and worksheet as next term's template in Canva and Gamma, so the work compounds instead of repeating.

None of this replaces teaching. It replaces the two hours of admin that used to follow teaching, which is the part teachers actually want back.
