What does official ElevenLabs charge?

ElevenLabs publishes Creator on its pricing page. As that page stands, Creator is $22 a month, with the first month at $11, and it includes 121,000 credits. Annual billing works out to $18.33 a month because you pay for 10 months and get 12. Pro, the step above Creator, is $99 a month with 600,000 credits. Prices exclude tax.

Creator includes professional voice cloning and a commercial licence. Free is personal use only, with a much smaller credit pool. Starter is the cheaper paid step and, on ElevenLabs' own comparison, is the tier for instant cloning rather than professional cloning.

A few rules from that pricing page and the billing docs that change how you should plan a month:

  • Credits are one shared pool. Text to speech, sound effects, dubbing and the other products all spend the same credits.
  • On multilingual v2 models, 1 character of text costs about 1 credit. A 1,000-character script is about 1,000 credits before you regenerate.
  • Unused credits on a paid plan can roll over for up to two months, capped so the balance does not grow forever. Cancel or downgrade and the unused credits are forfeited at the end of the cycle.
  • Regenerating because you disliked a take can be free in some cases, and charged in others. The button tells you before you spend.

Our rupee plans are prepaid access to a Creator-level private account. They are not an ElevenLabs invoice in your name. If you need the invoice, the API dashboard in your own company name, or a specific seat count, buy from ElevenLabs directly and use an international card.

How do you turn a script into a voiceover?

This is the workflow that fits a weekly video, not a studio manual.

  1. Write the script in short lines. One sentence per line is easier to re-roll than a huge paragraph.
  2. Sign in to the private account and open Text to Speech. Pick a stock voice first. Do not start with cloning.
  3. Generate one paragraph. If the accent or pace is wrong, change the voice or the punctuation before you generate the whole script.
  4. Download the audio. WAV or high-quality MP3 is the usual choice for editing.
  5. Put the file on a track in CapCut Pro or your editor. Cut breaths and bad takes. Add music underneath at a low volume.
  6. Export once. Re-exporting the same generation does not spend credits. A new generation does.

For a 10-minute narration you might be looking at roughly 8,000 to 12,000 characters, depending on how fast you write. At about one credit per character on the multilingual model, that is a small slice of a 121,000-credit official Creator pool, as long as you do not regenerate every line ten times. Dubbing and music spend much faster. ElevenLabs lists dubbing in the thousands of credits per minute. Do not dub a whole film on a Creator pool and expect it to last the month.

Voice cloning, consent and commercial use

Professional voice cloning is the feature people search for, and it is the one that gets accounts in trouble when it is abused.

Clone a voice only when you have the right to do it. That means your own voice, or a speaker who has agreed in a way you can show later. Do not clone a celebrity, a client, a teacher or a random clip from YouTube. ElevenLabs' commercial licence covers the audio you generate under the plan rules. It does not give you the right to impersonate someone.

A sensible order:

  • Use a stock voice for the first videos until the channel has a style.
  • Clone your own voice when you want every video to sound like you but you do not want to record.
  • Keep the original sample and the consent message with your project files.
  • If a client asks you to clone their voice, get the request in writing before you upload the sample.

Our account is private, so the cloned voice sits on that login. If the plan ends and you do not control the login forever, export the audio you need and do not leave client voice models as the only copy. For the difference between private and shared logins, read private vs shared vs invitation accounts.

Use ElevenLabs when the picture is stock footage, slides or gameplay and the voice carries the video. Use HeyGen when you need a face, a course lesson or a translated presenter. A lot of faceless channels never need HeyGen. A lot of course creators need both, and they should buy the one they will open this week first.
