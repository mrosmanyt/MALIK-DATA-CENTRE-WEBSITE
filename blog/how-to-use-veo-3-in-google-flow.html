Veo 3 is Google's AI video model, and Google Flow is the tool you use to make videos with it. The combination is powerful — and confusing the first time, because there is no "Veo app" to download and no standalone Veo subscription to buy. This guide walks through the whole process from Pakistan: opening Flow, using the free credits, generating your first clip, and knowing when to pay for a plan. The prices are in our Veo 3 price guide; this post is about the steps.

What Google Flow is

Google Flow is Google's AI filmmaking tool. You describe a scene in words, Flow sends it to Veo, and Veo returns a short video clip with motion and sound. Flow also keeps your clips organised into scenes, so a longer video is built scene by scene rather than in one impossible prompt. Think of Flow as the studio and Veo as the camera: you work in Flow, and Veo does the generating.

Access works through Flow credits. Any Google account can open Flow and receives a small free daily credit allowance, which is enough to test Veo. Regular or high-quality production needs a paid Google AI plan, because every generation spends credits. That is the whole pricing model, and the rest of this guide is built around it.

Before you start

  • A Google account. Any Gmail works for the free test; a paid plan is activated on your own Gmail too, so use the account you want the videos on.
  • A browser on a laptop or desktop. Flow is a web tool, and prompting, previewing and downloading clips are all easier on a big screen than on a phone.
  • A rough idea of the video. One sentence per scene is enough: who is in it, where they are, what happens, and the mood. Flow works best when the prompt is specific.
  • Nothing to pay yet. Do the free test first, and only buy a plan when the free daily allowance runs out before your ideas do.

Step by step: your first Veo clip

  1. Open Google Flow in your browser and sign in with your Google account. You will see the Flow workspace with your projects and your credit balance.
  2. Start a new project. Give it a name you will recognise later — the film title or the client's name, not "Untitled 1".
  3. Write your first prompt. Describe one scene, not a whole film: the subject, the setting, the action and the camera. Example structure: "A wolf pup in snowy pine forest at dawn, slow camera push-in, soft golden light, cinematic." Short and specific beats long and vague.
  4. Pick the generation mode. Flow offers a faster, cheaper mode for drafts and a full-quality mode for finals. Draft in the cheaper mode and only render the scenes you keep at full quality — that one habit saves most of your credits.
  5. Generate and wait. Veo renders the clip in the background. A few minutes is normal; do not regenerate the same prompt while the first one is still running, or you pay twice for the same scene.
  6. Preview, keep or retry. Watch the clip. If the motion is wrong, change one thing in the prompt — the action, or the camera — and generate again. Changing everything at once teaches you nothing about what fixed it.
  7. Download the clip and keep it in a project folder with the prompt that made it, so you can match the style in the next scene.

Free credits: how far do they go?

Any Google account gets a small free daily credit allowance in Flow. That is enough to test Veo properly: generate a few clips, learn how prompts behave, and decide whether video is worth paying for. It is not enough to produce a channel. The allowance refreshes daily, so a patient creator can build a small library over weeks, but anyone publishing regularly will outgrow it within the first month — which is the point at which a paid plan pays for itself.

Tips for your first videos

  • One scene, one prompt. Flow builds films scene by scene. A prompt that tries to tell a whole story gives you a confused clip.
  • Keep the style words constant. If scene one says "cinematic, golden light", say it in scene two as well, or the film will look like two different films.
  • Generate variations, not repeats. Two or three versions of the same scene give you a choice in the edit; the same prompt ten times gives you ten similar clips and an empty credit balance.
  • Sound matters. Veo 3 generates audio with the video. Write the sound into the prompt — "wind in the pines, distant wolf howl" — instead of adding it later.
  • Save your prompts. The prompt is the recipe. A clip you cannot reproduce is a clip you cannot continue.

Mistakes beginners make

  • Rendering everything at full quality. Draft cheap, render once. This is the most expensive mistake in Flow.
  • Buying Ultra before testing the free tier. Try the free daily allowance first. If you cannot finish a test video on it, you know what you need; if you can, you just saved Rs 2,500.
  • Paying for a "standalone Veo" plan. Google does not sell one. Anyone selling "Veo 3 subscription" on its own is selling a Google AI plan with a different name — check what you are actually getting.
  • Sharing logins for client work. A shared plan is fine for testing, but client videos belong on your own Gmail plan or a dedicated slot. Our shared vs private accounts guide explains why.
