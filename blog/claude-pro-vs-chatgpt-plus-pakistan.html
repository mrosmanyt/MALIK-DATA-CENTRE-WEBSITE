Claude Pro and ChatGPT Plus are the two flagship chatbot subscriptions, and in Pakistan the choice between them is unusually clean: they cost exactly the same. Both are $20 a month on the web, and both are Rs 4,900 a month on the Pakistan App Store. So the question is never "which is cheaper" — it is "which one fits the work you actually do". This guide compares them on writing, coding, everyday use and limits, then shows the cheaper routes in Pakistan, including the budget seat most buyers should look at first.

The price, first: both cost the same

Start with the numbers, because they remove price from the decision entirely. Claude Pro costs $20 a month on Anthropic's website, and ChatGPT Plus costs $20 a month on OpenAI's website. On the Pakistan App Store both are Rs 4,900 a month. Neither has a cheaper official monthly tier in Pakistan, and neither is sold as a discounted yearly plan by the company itself.

That matters because most comparisons online are really price comparisons in disguise — one tool looks better only because it costs less where the writer lives. Here, the official prices are identical, so the rest of this guide is about capability, not cost. The full price breakdowns live in our Claude Pro price guide and our ChatGPT Plus price guide.

What each one does best

Both are general chatbots that write, summarise, answer questions and help with code. The difference is emphasis. Claude is built around long, careful text: it holds long documents in context, writes in a steadier voice, and is unusually good at working through a codebase or a long brief without losing the thread. ChatGPT is built around breadth: voice conversation, image generation, file handling, and a large ecosystem of apps and integrations that put the chatbot inside other tools.

For a buyer, the practical test is a normal working day. If your day is mostly long documents — a thesis chapter, a client report, a 2,000-line file, a contract you need summarised accurately — Claude's strengths show up first. If your day is many small jobs — a quick email, a voice question while driving, an image for a post, a spreadsheet explained — ChatGPT's breadth shows up first.

Where Claude Pro wins

Long writing. Claude keeps a consistent voice across long pieces and is good at structured writing: reports, essays, documentation and fiction drafts. Writers who work in long sessions notice this first.

Reading long documents. Drop in a long PDF or a set of notes and Claude works through the whole thing, summarises it accurately and answers questions that need detail from page 40, not just the introduction. This is the feature students and researchers feel most.

Coding. Claude is widely regarded as the stronger coding model of the two for everyday development — writing new code, refactoring, and debugging with the full file in view. Developers who build with AI daily usually name Claude first. Note the distinction, though: Claude Pro is the chat subscription. Developers who want Claude's models inside their own apps buy Claude API tokens instead, which are price on request — the Claude API guide explains the difference.

Where ChatGPT Plus wins

Everyday chat and voice. ChatGPT Plus is the better everyday companion: quick questions, voice mode for hands-free conversation, and a model that is good across a very wide range of small tasks rather than specialised in one.

Images and multimodal work. Plus generates images, reads images you upload, and works with files in a way that suits social media work, presentations and design-adjacent jobs. If your work involves pictures as often as words, ChatGPT has the edge.

The ecosystem. ChatGPT connects to more apps and services, and its Plus plan is the one most tutorials, templates and workflows assume. If you follow YouTube tutorials or use AI inside other tools, the instructions you find will usually be written for ChatGPT.

The budget door in Pakistan. OpenAI's Go plan is the practical reason many Pakistani buyers never pay $20 at all: ChatGPT Go at Rs 2,200 for 6 months covers everyday chat for a fraction of the Plus price, and our ChatGPT Plus vs ChatGPT Go guide settles exactly what you give up.

Limits both plans share

Neither plan is unlimited, and buyers should know that before paying $20 a month. Both throttle heavy use: after a certain amount of messages or long sessions in a short window, each one slows you down or asks you to wait. The exact limits change over time and are not printed as a single number, so treat any seller who promises "unlimited Claude Pro" or "unlimited ChatGPT Plus" with suspicion — the official plans do not work that way.

Both also need a stable connection and a modern browser or app, and both store your chats on your own account. Neither plan is a shared login when bought officially: it is your account, your history, your data. That is the standard to hold any reseller to — if a "Pro" offer in Pakistan is far below $20 a month, ask exactly what access type it is before paying, and read our guide to private vs shared vs invitation accounts first.

Which should you pick?

Pick Claude Pro if your main work is writing, research or code: long documents, theses, client reports, or daily development work. You will feel the difference in the first week, and the $20 is paying for depth.

Pick ChatGPT Plus if your main work is varied: a bit of writing, a bit of images, voice questions, quick research, and using AI inside other apps. You will feel the difference in breadth, and the $20 is paying for range.

Pick neither yet if you are a student or a light user. Start with ChatGPT Go at Rs 2,200 for 6 months — about Rs 367 a month — and upgrade to a $20 plan only when you hit Go's limits in real work. Most students never do. Our ChatGPT Go vs Gemini Pro for students guide makes the same point for the Google side.

Pick Claude API instead of Claude Pro if you are a developer building with Claude's models in your own app or workflow. A chat login is the wrong product for that job — API tokens are priced on request, and the team can quote them on WhatsApp.

The cheaper routes in Pakistan

Paying $20 a month — Rs 4,900 a month on the App Store — is real money in Pakistan, and most buyers should check the cheaper routes before committing. ChatGPT Go at Rs 2,200 for 6 months is the obvious one on the ChatGPT side. On the Google side, Gemini Pro at Rs 1,500 for 12 months on your own Gmail is the cheapest full-year flagship plan in the catalog, and Gemini Plus at Rs 600 for 12 months is cheaper still. None of these is Claude or ChatGPT Plus, and none pretends to be — but for everyday chat, study and writing, any of them covers the job for a tenth of the price.

The honest way to decide is to try the cheap seat first. Use ChatGPT Go or Gemini Pro for a month of real work. If you keep hitting a wall that only Claude's long-document handling or ChatGPT Plus's full feature set would fix, then pay the $20 — and you will know exactly what you are paying for.

Claude Pro is the stronger pick for most coding work — long codebases, careful rewrites and debugging. ChatGPT Plus is still capable, and its wider ecosystem (voice, images, apps) suits people who code only part of the day.
