The price, first: Rs 2,500 a month on your email

NordVPN costs Rs 2,500 for one month of access, activated on your own email. That is the whole plan: one price, one month, your email. There is no longer plan in the catalog and no shared-login version — you pay per month and the access is yours, not a seat on someone else's account.

For comparison, NordVPN's own official price is $14.99 a month on its monthly plan, which is roughly Rs 4,200 at typical card rates — before any foreign-transaction charges your bank adds. The store price of Rs 2,500 is lower than the official monthly price and, just as importantly, it is payable in rupees through EasyPaisa, JazzCash, NayaPay, UPaisa, Meezan Bank or bank transfer, or USDT from abroad. No international card is needed at all.

The price is monthly, so it suits people who need a VPN for a defined stretch — a month of remote work, a trip abroad, a project that needs a foreign IP — rather than a year-long commitment. If you only need a VPN for a few weeks, paying Rs 2,500 once beats paying for a year of anything.

What "monthly access on your email" means

"Activated on your email" is the important part of this plan, and it is worth understanding before you buy. The NordVPN access is set up on an email address you provide, so the account belongs to your month of use rather than being a shared login passed between buyers. You log in with your own credentials, your browsing stays yours, and nobody else is on the same account while you use it.

That is a real difference from the cheapest VPN options in the catalog. Surfshark starts at Rs 400 a month on a shared login, which is far cheaper but means the account is shared. NordVPN at Rs 2,500 is the private option: your email, your month, no sharing. If privacy is the reason you want a VPN in the first place, that distinction matters more than the price gap.

Delivery is the same as every other tool in the store: message us on WhatsApp, confirm the plan and today's price, pay, and the activation is usually done within 5 to 30 minutes after payment is confirmed.

What you get with NordVPN

NordVPN is a full VPN service, not a browser extension or a free proxy. When it is on, your internet traffic is encrypted and routed through one of NordVPN's servers, so your real IP address and location are hidden from the websites you visit and from anyone watching the local network — useful on public Wi-Fi in cafes, hotels and airports, and for anyone whose work or study needs a stable foreign IP.

The plan includes the standard NordVPN protections: a kill switch that cuts your internet if the VPN connection drops, so your real IP never leaks mid-session, and a strict no-logs policy, which means the service does not keep records of what you do online. It works on Windows, macOS, Android and iOS, so the same month of access covers your laptop and your phone.

It also unblocks the practical things people in Pakistan actually buy a VPN for: streaming libraries that are not available locally, websites and services that block Pakistani IPs, and apps that price differently by region. One month is enough to find out whether those use cases matter to you before you commit to anything longer.

Is Rs 2,500 a month worth it?

It depends on how much you will use it. At Rs 2,500 a month, NordVPN costs about Rs 83 a day — less than a cup of chai from a cafe, for a service that encrypts everything you do online. If you work remotely, handle client data, or spend your days on public Wi-Fi, that is easy to justify. If you only open a VPN once a week to watch a geo-blocked show, the Rs 400 Surfshark shared login does the same job for far less, and you should buy that instead.

The other way to judge it is against the official price. NordVPN's own monthly plan is $14.99, and paying it directly means an international card, a dollar charge, and whatever your bank adds on top. Rs 2,500 in PKR on WhatsApp is simply cheaper and simpler for a buyer in Pakistan. The only honest caveat is that a longer official plan is cheaper per month than any monthly plan — but longer official plans also need a card and a year of commitment, which is exactly what many buyers here do not have.

Who should buy NordVPN

Buy NordVPN if you want a private VPN for a month — remote workers, freelancers with foreign clients, travellers, and anyone who wants their own email on the account rather than a shared login.

Buy Surfshark instead if the budget is the deciding factor and a shared login does not bother you. At Rs 400 a month it is the cheapest VPN in the catalog by a wide margin.

Buy HMA instead if you need a VPN for the whole year and would rather pay once. Rs 5,500 for a yearly key is the cheapest per-month rate of the three if you actually use all twelve months.

Buy none yet if you only need a VPN for a single task, like checking one blocked website. Free VPNs exist for that, and a paid month is overkill for a five-minute job.
