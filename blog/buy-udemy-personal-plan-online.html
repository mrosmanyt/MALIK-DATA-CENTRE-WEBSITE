What to do in the 30 days

One month is short. Treat it like a project with an end date.

  1. Pick one skill, not twelve. A freelancer learning a tool they will use on the next job, or a career switcher sampling a topic, can finish a path. Someone who bookmarks forty courses will finish none.
  2. On day one, open the account and search for that topic. If the course is missing or locked behind a separate purchase, screenshot the course page the same day, while the 7-day warranty is the relevant line.
  3. Download exercise files to your own computer as you go. When the month ends, the login may not be yours to keep. Notes and files on your disk are what remain.
  4. Watch with the account’s rules in mind. A subscription course is for the subscriber. Sharing the login with a group burns the month and can get the account restricted by Udemy, which we cannot override.
  5. Put the end date in your calendar on the day the login arrives. Day 29 is late to start a 20-hour course.

Certificates, completion badges and whether a course offers one at all follow that course’s own rules. This page does not say every Personal Plan course issues a certificate, and a certificate is not a degree. Finish the work if you want the badge. Do not buy the month only for a logo to paste on a profile.

The delivery policy says most orders are ready within 5–30 minutes after payment is confirmed. The refund policy covers an order we cannot deliver, and problems that show up inside the warranty. Seven days is the warranty printed on this row. A course you simply did not like, after you watched it, is not the same as a login that never opened. Read the policy before you pay if that difference matters to you.
